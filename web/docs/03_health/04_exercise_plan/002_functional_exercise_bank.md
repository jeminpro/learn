# **“stay capable for life” exercise bank**.

## ⚖️ 1. Balance

### Basic → harder

* Two-foot narrow stance
* Tandem stance (one foot directly in front of the other)
* Heel-to-toe walking
* Single-leg stand
* Single-leg stand with head turns
* Single-leg stand while reaching
* Single-leg stand with eyes closed *(only near support)*
* Clock reaches — standing on one leg and reaching the other foot in different directions
* Single-leg hip hinge/reach
* Controlled step-down
* Step-up and controlled step-down
* Sideways stepping
* Grapevine/side crossover walking

**Especially valuable:** single-leg stand, heel-to-toe walking, controlled step-down and reaching.

# 🧎 3. Floor mobility & independence

This is **particularly useful for your long-term goal**.

* Sit down onto floor and stand back up
* Floor → kneeling
* Kneeling → standing
* Half-kneeling position
* Half-kneeling → standing
* Side-sit → cross-legged
* Cross-legged → side-sit
* Cross-legged → kneeling
* Supported deep squat → floor
* Floor → supported squat → standing
* Rolling from back → side → sitting
* Sit-to-floor using one hand
* Sit-to-floor without hands
* Floor-to-stand without furniture *(when comfortable)*

You don't need to make these exhausting. **Practising the movements is the training.**

* Single-leg stand
* Heel raises
* Slow heel raises
* Single-leg heel raises
* Toe raises

* Short-foot exercise
* Foot arch contractions
* Heel-to-toe walking
* Walking on heels
* Walking on toes
* Side stepping
* Forward/backward stepping
* Controlled step-downs
* Ankle inversion/eversion with band

# 🧠 5. Coordination

These are great for keeping movement skills sharp.

* Marching in place
* High-knee marching
* Opposite hand-to-knee marching
* Cross-body marching
* Side-to-side stepping
* Forward/backward stepping
* Step-touch
* Grapevine
* Heel-to-toe walking
* March + arm movements
* Alternating toe taps
* Clock stepping
* Step in different directions on command
* Single-leg reach in different directions
* Walking while turning the head

You can gradually make these more challenging by adding **direction changes, arm movements or faster reactions**.

---

# 🚶 6. Functional movement patterns

These are movements that resemble things you actually do in life.

* Sit-to-stand
* Squat
* Supported deep squat
* Step-up
* Step-down
* Side step
* Reverse step
* Lateral squat
* Hip hinge
* Reach-to-floor
* Reach overhead
* Carry and walk
* Pick something up from the floor
* Put something onto a high shelf
* Push/pull an object
* Turn and reach
* Walk backwards
* Walk sideways

Some of these overlap with your strength workouts, which is completely fine.

---

# 👜 7. Carrying & everyday capability

Even though you might classify these as strength, they're **very functional**.

* Farmer carry
* Suitcase carry
* Front carry
* Carry while walking
* Carry up/down stairs
* Carry and turn
* Carry and step over an obstacle

You can use relatively light weights and focus on **walking well and maintaining posture**.

---

# 🪜 8. Step & stair skills

Very useful for ageing well.

* Low step-ups
* Alternating step-ups
* Controlled step-downs
* Side step-ups
* Step-up + knee lift
* Stair walking
* Slow stair descent
* One-step-at-a-time → alternating stairs
* Step over a low obstacle
* Step around an obstacle

**Controlled stair descent** is particularly worth practising because it requires strength, balance and coordination.

---

# ⚡ 9. Reaction & agility — later

You don't need these immediately, but they're excellent additions as you become more capable.

* Quick step-touch
* Fast marching
* Direction-change walking
* Step forward/back on cue
* Side-step on cue
* Toe taps
* Low obstacle stepping
* Multi-directional stepping
* Catching/throwing a soft ball while standing
* Catching while standing on a slightly narrower stance
* Reaction to a visual/auditory cue

You **don't need jumping or burpees** to develop these qualities.

# 🫁 11. Breathing / relaxation

Useful as your cool-down:

* Diaphragmatic breathing
* Slow nasal breathing
* Long-exhale breathing
* Relaxed lying-down breathing
* Gentle body scan

You could also use **Tai Chi-style slow movements** here if you enjoy them. It can combine mobility, balance, coordination and controlled breathing in one session.

---

## ⭐ If you don't want a huge list every day

You **don't need to pick 10–15 exercises**.

For a 20–30 minute session, I'd usually pick:

**1 balance + 2 mobility + 1 coordination + 1 functional movement + 1 floor exercise**

For example:

**Tuesday**

* Single-leg stand — 2 × 30 sec
* Knee-to-wall — 10/side
* 90/90 switches — 8/side
* Cross-body marching — 2 × 30 sec
* Step-ups — 2 × 8/side
* Floor-to-stand practice — 5 min

Then Thursday could be completely different.

### And over the year...

You can gradually progress from:

**stable → unstable**
**slow → faster**
**simple → coordinated**
**supported → unsupported**
**small range → larger range**
**two legs → one leg**
**predictable → reactive**

That's what will make your **functional training genuinely useful for your 50s, 60s, 70s and beyond**, rather than simply being a collection of stretches.
