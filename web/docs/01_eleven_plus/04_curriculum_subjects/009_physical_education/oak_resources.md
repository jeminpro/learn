# Oak Teaching Resource

## Scope

This resource covers **Physical Education knowledge through the end of Year 5**, drawing on:

* The England National Curriculum for PE.
* Oak National Academy's Primary PE curriculum from Year 1 through Year 5.
* Oak's progression in movement competence, physical fitness, tactics, decision-making, teamwork, performance and evaluation.
* The practical knowledge most useful for an **FSCE-style multiple-choice or single-answer question**.

The emphasis is on **knowing and recognising PE concepts**, not on requiring a child to be able to perform every sporting skill.

---

# 1. Fundamental Movement Skills

## 1.1 Running

Running is a locomotion skill used in athletics, games and many other activities.

### Key ideas

* Running involves repeated steps with a flight phase between steps.
* Arms and legs should work together.
* Good running technique helps a runner move efficiently.
* Different situations require different speeds.
* A **sprint** is a short-distance run performed at high speed.
* **Acceleration** means increasing speed.
* A runner may accelerate at the start of a race.
* In games, running into **space** can help a player receive a pass or avoid a defender.
* Running technique can be adapted depending on the task: sprinting, jogging, changing direction or running as part of a relay.

### Sprinting

Important ideas:

* Start with the body prepared to accelerate.
* Drive the arms and legs in coordination.
* Look ahead.
* Build or maintain speed appropriately.
* Coordinate arm and leg movements.

### Important vocabulary

| Word           | Meaning                                                                                  |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Run**        | Move quickly by taking alternating steps with periods when both feet are off the ground. |
| **Sprint**     | Run at very high speed over a short distance.                                            |
| **Accelerate** | Increase speed.                                                                          |
| **Decelerate** | Decrease speed.                                                                          |
| **Speed**      | How quickly something moves.                                                             |
| **Locomotion** | Movement from one place to another.                                                      |
| **Stride**     | The distance covered by one step or cycle of running movement.                           |
| **Relay**      | A race in which members of a team take turns completing sections.                        |

Oak develops running from basic running technique in Year 1 towards acceleration, competitive running, team running and sprint evaluation by Year 5. ([Oak National Academy][2])

# 2. Jumping, Hopping and Leaping

## 2.1 Jumping

A jump involves:

1. **Take-off**
2. Flight
3. **Landing**

Jumping can be used to achieve:

* height
* distance
* movement over an obstacle
* movement in a game

### Safe and effective landing

A controlled landing is important.

A child should:

* bend the knees;
* control the body;
* maintain balance;
* land safely rather than simply trying to jump as far or as high as possible.

### Jumping for distance

To jump further:

* use an effective take-off;
* generate appropriate force;
* coordinate the arms and legs;
* control the landing.

### Jumping for height

The technique and force used may differ from a jump for distance.

### Important distinction

**A longer jump is not automatically a better jump.**

A controlled, balanced landing is part of good technique.

---

## 2.2 Hop

A **hop** takes off and lands on the **same foot**.

Example:

> Right foot → take off → land on right foot.

---

## 2.3 Leap

A **leap** generally involves taking off from one foot and landing on the other.

Example:

> Right foot → take off → land on left foot.

---

## 2.4 Jump

A jump commonly involves taking off from two feet and landing on two feet, although the exact movement depends on the activity.

### Important vocabulary

| Word         | Meaning                                            |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Jump**     | Propel the body into the air.                      |
| **Hop**      | Take off and land on the same foot.                |
| **Leap**     | Travel through the air from one foot to the other. |
| **Take-off** | The moment the body leaves the ground.             |
| **Landing**  | Returning to the ground after a jump.              |
| **Flight**   | The period when the body is airborne.              |
| **Height**   | How high something reaches.                        |
| **Distance** | How far something travels.                         |

Oak Year 1 explicitly develops jumping in different directions, at different heights and distances, together with hopping and skipping; later years develop jumping and flight technique further. ([Oak National Academy][2])

# 3. Throwing

Throwing sends an object through the air.

The outcome of a throw depends on factors such as:

* direction;
* release point;
* force;
* angle;
* accuracy;
* technique.

## 3.1 Throwing for distance

The aim is to make the object travel as far as possible.

Greater force can increase distance, but simply using more force does not always produce the best result.

## 3.2 Throwing for accuracy

The aim is to hit a particular target.

Accuracy and distance are **different goals**.

A powerful throw may travel far but miss the target.

### Types of throw

Common throwing actions include:

* underarm throw;
* overarm throw;
* push/pass;
* chest pass;
* overhead throw.

### Important vocabulary

| Word         | Meaning                                                      |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Throw**    | Send an object through the air using the body.               |
| **Underarm** | Throwing with the arm moving below shoulder level.           |
| **Overarm**  | Throwing with the arm moving above or around shoulder level. |
| **Release**  | The moment an object leaves the hand.                        |
| **Force**    | A push or pull that can change movement.                     |
| **Accuracy** | How close an attempt is to its intended target.              |
| **Distance** | How far the object travels.                                  |
| **Target**   | The object or area you are trying to hit.                    |

# 4. Catching

Catching means receiving and controlling an object.

A successful catch requires:

* watching the object;
* judging its speed and direction;
* moving into the object's path when necessary;
* positioning the hands appropriately;
* absorbing and controlling the object.

### Catching can be combined with other movements

For example:

> Run → catch → turn → pass.

This is important because PE skills are increasingly combined rather than practised only in isolation.

---

# 5. Sending and Receiving

**Sending** means moving an object towards another person, target or area.

**Receiving** means controlling an object sent to you.

Sending and receiving can include:

* throwing and catching;
* passing;
* rolling;
* kicking;
* striking.

The choice of technique depends on:

* distance;
* target;
* speed;
* pressure from opponents;
* available space.

# 6. Coordination

## What is coordination?

**Coordination** means different parts of the body working together effectively and at the appropriate time.

Examples:

* arms and legs working together while sprinting;
* coordinating hands and eyes when catching;
* coordinating legs and arms when jumping;
* coordinating movement with a partner.

### Hand-eye coordination

This involves using visual information to control hand movements.

Examples:

* catching a ball;
* hitting a ball with a racket;
* striking a cricket ball.

### Important vocabulary

| Word             | Meaning                                                        |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Coordination** | Body parts working together effectively and at the right time. |
| **Reaction**     | Responding to something that happens.                          |
| **Timing**       | Performing an action at the appropriate moment.                |
| **Rhythm**       | A repeated pattern of movement or timing.                      |

Oak identifies coordination as an important movement competence and explicitly connects it with running, jumping and ball skills. ([Oak National Academy][2])

# 7. Balance

## What is balance?

**Balance** is the ability to keep the body steady while stationary or moving.

### Static balance

Keeping still while maintaining stability.

Examples:

* standing on one leg;
* holding a gymnastics position.

### Dynamic balance

Maintaining balance while moving.

Examples:

* walking along a line;
* moving through a sequence;
* changing direction while staying controlled.

## Base of support

The **base of support** is the area underneath the body that supports it.

Generally:

* a wider base can make a position more stable;
* a narrower base makes balance more challenging.

### Important vocabulary

| Word                | Meaning                                         |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Balance**         | Ability to remain stable while still or moving. |
| **Static balance**  | Balance while staying still.                    |
| **Dynamic balance** | Balance while moving.                           |
| **Stability**       | Ability to remain controlled and steady.        |
| **Base of support** | The area supporting the body.                   |

---

# 8. Agility

**Agility** is the ability to move quickly and effectively, particularly when changing:

* direction;
* speed;
* body position.

Agility is especially important in games.

Examples:

* dodging a defender;
* changing direction to receive a pass;
* moving around an opponent;
* reacting to a moving ball.

### Agility vs speed

* **Speed** = how quickly you move.
* **Agility** = how effectively you can move/change direction, often quickly.

Year 2 Oak specifically develops agility, balance and coordination together. ([Oak National Academy][1])

---

# 9. Strength

**Strength** is the ability to produce force.

Strength can be used when:

* jumping;
* throwing;
* pushing;
* holding a gymnastics position;
* running;
* striking an object.

Strength is different from speed.

A person can be strong without being the fastest runner.

---

# 10. Flexibility

**Flexibility** is the ability to move a joint through an appropriate range of movement.

Flexibility can help with:

* gymnastics;
* dance;
* reaching;
* movement sequences;
* athletic activities.

### Important distinction

**Flexibility ≠ strength**

* Strength relates to producing force.
* Flexibility relates to range of movement.

Oak develops strength and flexibility explicitly through its KS2 health, gymnastics and athletics units. ([Oak National Academy][1])

---

# 11. Control

**Control** means being able to regulate:

* body position;
* speed;
* force;
* movement.

Good control is important when:

* landing a jump;
* balancing;
* catching;
* changing direction;
* performing gymnastics;
* performing a dance sequence.

A movement can be powerful but poorly controlled.

---

# 12. Technique

**Technique** is the method used to perform a particular skill.

Examples:

* running technique;
* jumping technique;
* throwing technique;
* catching technique;
* swimming technique;
* striking technique.

Different activities require different techniques.

### Technique vs tactic

**Technique** = *how* you perform a skill.

**Tactic** = *when/why* you choose to use a skill or strategy.

Example:

> Passing is a skill/technique.
> Passing into space to avoid a defender is a tactical decision.

# 13. Physical Fitness and Healthy Activity

Regular physical activity can help:

* develop physical skills;
* improve fitness;
* develop confidence;
* support health;
* maintain an active lifestyle.

## Fitness

**Fitness** is the ability to cope with the physical demands of activity.

Different activities require different physical qualities.

For example:

| Activity      | Important qualities                      |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Sprint        | Speed, power, coordination               |
| Long activity | Stamina/endurance                        |
| Gymnastics    | Strength, flexibility, balance, control  |
| Dance         | Coordination, flexibility, control       |
| Team game     | Fitness, agility, coordination and skill |

---

# 14. Warm-up and Preparation

A warm-up prepares the body and attention for physical activity.

A suitable warm-up can involve:

* gentle movement;
* gradually increasing activity;
* movement relevant to the activity.

The purpose is to prepare rather than immediately perform maximal effort.

Oak includes health and wellbeing units from Year 1 onward and develops understanding of physical activity, fitness, strength, flexibility and wellbeing through Year 5. ([Oak National Academy][1])

# 15. Invasion Games

An **invasion game** involves two teams attempting to attack a target while preventing the opposing team from doing the same.

Examples include:

* football;
* hockey;
* basketball;
* netball.

The fundamental ideas are:

* attack;
* defence;
* possession;
* space;
* support;
* passing;
* scoring;
* denying space.

---

# 16. Attack

An attacker tries to:

* maintain possession;
* move towards a target;
* create a scoring opportunity;
* move into useful space;
* support teammates.

## Using space

An attacker can:

* spread out;
* move into an empty area;
* make a run;
* change direction;
* move away from a defender.

### Support

**Support** means moving into a useful position so a teammate has an option.

A player does not have to have the ball to contribute to an attack.

---

# 17. Defence

A defender tries to:

* prevent scoring;
* delay an attacker;
* protect space;
* mark an opponent;
* intercept passes;
* regain possession.

## Marking

**Marking** means staying close enough to an opponent to limit their options.

Marking does **not** automatically mean physical contact.

---

# 18. Intercepting

**Intercept** means stopping or taking a pass before it reaches the intended teammate.

Example:

> A defender reads where a pass is going and moves into its path.

---

# 19. Possession

**Possession** means having control of the ball or playing object.

When possession changes:

> Attack → defence

or

> Defence → attack

This change is called a **transition**.

---

# 20. Tactics

A **tactic** is a planned choice used to help achieve a goal during a game.

Examples:

* pass into space;
* spread out;
* mark an opponent;
* move into a scoring position;
* protect an area;
* change direction;
* switch quickly from attack to defence.

### Skill vs tactic

| Skill  | Tactic                                        |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Pass   | Pass into space                               |
| Run    | Run into an empty area                        |
| Throw  | Throw towards a teammate in a better position |
| Move   | Move to support a teammate                    |
| Defend | Mark an opponent                              |

Oak's KS2 progression develops pupils from basic attacking and defending principles in Year 3 towards maintaining possession, stopping attacks, creating scoring opportunities and denying space by Year 5. ([Oak National Academy][1])

# 22. Net and Wall Games

Net and wall games involve sending an object over or against a net/wall so that an opponent cannot successfully return it.

Examples include:

* tennis;
* pickleball;
* badminton.

Oak introduces tactics for overcoming opponents through net and wall games in Years 3–5, including pickleball in Year 4 and tennis in Year 5. ([Oak National Academy][1])

## Key ideas

* send the ball/shuttle into space;
* make it difficult for an opponent to return;
* recover to a useful position;
* anticipate the opponent;
* vary direction;
* vary speed;
* aim away from the opponent.

## Important vocabulary

| Word         | Meaning                                                                |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Opponent** | The person or team you are playing against.                            |
| **Rally**    | A sequence of shots in which players return the object to one another. |
| **Serve**    | The action that starts play.                                           |
| **Return**   | Sending the ball/shuttle back to the opponent.                         |
| **Court**    | The marked playing area.                                               |
| **Net**      | The barrier separating opposing sides.                                 |

# 23. Striking and Fielding Games

Striking and fielding games involve one side striking an object and the other side fielding it.

Examples include:

* cricket;
* rounders.

Oak develops striking and fielding from batting and fielding principles in Year 3 through tactical play in rounders in Year 4 and cricket skills in Year 5. ([Oak National Academy][1])

## Striker/batter

The striker tries to:

* hit the ball effectively;
* find space;
* score runs;
* make it difficult for fielders to return the ball quickly.

## Fielders

Fielders try to:

* retrieve the ball;
* prevent runs;
* catch the ball;
* return it quickly;
* put pressure on the batter/runners.

* tuck;
* pike;
* straddle;

# 27. Gymnastics — Sequences

A **sequence** is a series of movements performed in a planned order.

A sequence can include:

> Balance → travel → jump → turn → balance

A good sequence may demonstrate:

* control;
* balance;
* fluency;
* timing;
* appropriate transitions;
* body tension.

---

# 28. Symmetry and Asymmetry

## Symmetry

A movement or shape is symmetrical when the two sides correspond or mirror one another.

Example:

> Both arms extended equally to the sides.

## Asymmetry

A movement or shape is asymmetrical when the two sides are different.

Example:

> One arm raised while the other is lowered.

---

# 29. Counterbalance

A **counterbalance** occurs when two people balance against each other using opposing forces.

The partners rely on each other to maintain balance.

---

# 30. Counter-tension

**Counter-tension** involves two people creating tension by pulling away from each other while maintaining a balanced position.

The forces help create stability.

---

# 31. Flight in Gymnastics

**Flight** refers to a period when the gymnast is airborne.

Important ideas:

* take-off;
* body shape;
* spatial awareness;
* control in the air;
* safe landing.

A controlled landing is important.

# 38. Personal Best

A **personal best** is the best result an individual has achieved in a particular activity.

Examples:

* fastest previous sprint;
* furthest previous jump;
* furthest throw.

A personal best is compared with the person's **own previous performance**, not necessarily another person's result.

---

# 39. Comparing Performance

Performance can be compared using evidence such as:

* time;
* distance;
* score;
* accuracy;
* number of successful attempts;
* quality of technique;
* control.

For a fair comparison, keep important conditions consistent.

For example:

* same distance;
* same starting point;
* same equipment;
* same basic rules.

Oak explicitly develops comparison, evaluation and improvement through its PE progression, while the National Curriculum requires pupils to compare performances with previous ones and demonstrate improvement towards a personal best. ([Jemin Pro][3])

---

# 40. Evaluation

To **evaluate** a performance means to judge it using evidence.

A useful evaluation can identify:

1. What went well?
2. What could be improved?
3. What specific change should be tried?

### Good feedback

> "Keep your eyes on the target before releasing."

### Weak feedback

> "Do better."

Useful feedback should give the performer something specific to change.

# 44. Problem Solving

1. Understand the problem.
2. Suggest possible solutions.
3. Try a solution.
4. Observe the result.

# 45. Decision Making

Decision making means selecting an appropriate action from available options.

Examples:

* Which direction should I run?
* Should I pass or move?
* Which space is safest?
* Which route should the team take?
* Should I attack or defend?
* Where should I send the ball?

Good decisions depend on recognising the situation.

# 46. Swimming and Water Safety

Swimming is part of the National Curriculum PE requirements.

By the end of KS2, pupils should be taught to:

* swim competently, confidently and proficiently for at least **25 metres**;
* use a range of strokes effectively;
* perform safe self-rescue in different water-based situations. ([GOV.UK][4])

Oak provides swimming and water-safety units that can be selected according to pupil ability and experience. ([Oak National Academy][1])

## Core aquatic concepts

### Floating

Keeping the body supported by the water.

### Propulsion

Using arms and/or legs to move through water.

### Stroke

A particular method of moving through water.

Examples:

* front crawl;
* backstroke;
* breaststroke.

### Water safety

Important principles include:

* recognising hazards;
* following water-safety instructions;
* responding appropriately in an emergency;
* knowing that different water environments can present different risks.

### Self-rescue

Self-rescue means using appropriate actions to keep yourself safe if you encounter difficulty in water.

| **Transition**      | Change from one phase/role to another, such as attack to defence.                   |

# 53. High-Value Comparisons

These distinctions are particularly useful for multiple-choice questions.

| Do not confuse                   | Difference                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Speed vs agility**             | Speed is how quickly you move; agility involves effective movement and changes of direction.     |
| **Strength vs flexibility**      | Strength is producing force; flexibility is range of movement.                                   |
| **Skill vs tactic**              | A skill is the action; a tactic is the decision about how/when to use it.                        |
| **Attack vs defence**            | Attack attempts to score/progress; defence attempts to prevent progress.                         |
| **Accuracy vs distance**         | Accuracy is closeness to the target; distance is how far something travels.                      |
| **Hop vs leap**                  | A hop lands on the same foot; a leap generally lands on the opposite foot.                       |
| **Static vs dynamic balance**    | Static balance is still; dynamic balance occurs while moving.                                    |
| **Performance vs personal best** | Performance is an individual attempt; personal best is the individual's best previous result.    |
| **Feedback vs evaluation**       | Feedback gives useful information for improvement; evaluation judges performance using evidence. |
| **Space vs possession**          | Space is an area available for movement; possession means controlling the ball/object.           |
| **Marking vs intercepting**      | Marking limits an opponent's options; intercepting stops/takes a pass.                           |
| **Attack vs transition**         | Attack is a phase of play; transition is the change between phases.                              |

# 54. Common FSCE Traps

## Trap 1 — More force is always better

**False.**

More force can increase distance but may reduce accuracy.

---

## Trap 2 — The longest jump is always the best jump

**False.**

Technique, control and a safe landing also matter.

---

## Trap 3 — Marking means grabbing the opponent

**False.**

Marking means limiting an opponent's options while following the rules.

---

## Trap 4 — A player must have the ball to help attack

**False.**

Moving into space and supporting a teammate can be useful without possession.

---

## Trap 5 — Winning proves improvement

**False.**

Improvement can be demonstrated by comparing an individual's current performance with previous performances.

---

## Trap 6 — Strength and flexibility mean the same thing

**False.**

Strength relates to producing force; flexibility relates to range of movement.

---

## Trap 7 — Speed and agility are the same

**False.**

Agility involves effective movement and changes of direction.

---

## Trap 8 — A tactic and a skill are the same

**False.**

Passing is a skill; deciding to pass into space to avoid a defender is a tactical decision.

---

## Trap 9 — Balance only means standing still

**False.**

Balance can be static or dynamic.

---

## Trap 10 — The same technique works for every physical activity

**False.**

Technique depends on the activity and the goal.
