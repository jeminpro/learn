6. **B** — More distance does not solve a problem of aim.
7. **C** — The catch is practised without the running movement.
8. **C** — A run followed by a jump links the two listed skills.
9. **A** — Technique and control matter when judging a movement.
10. **C** — Coordination of body parts supports efficient running.
11. **A** — Catching is receiving a moving object.
12. **D** — Reducing excessive force may make the throw easier to control.
13. **B** — Moving to a suitable position helps you receive it.
14. **D** — Moving between markers involves running.
15. **B** — The measured activity ends at the finish marker.
16. **B** — A jump includes take off and landing.
17. **D** — The goal names a measurable skill under the same conditions.
18. **A** — The two skills move an object in opposite directions relative to the player.
19. **C** — The description includes a run followed by a catch.
20. **A** — Suitable equipment supports safe practice; skill is still needed.

### Flexibility strength technique control and balance

21. **D** — Balance is the ability to keep a stable body position.
22. **A** — Flexibility concerns comfortable movement range.
23. **A** — The push requires the muscles to produce force.
24. **B** — Technique refers to how a skill is carried out.
25. **C** — Holding a steady one-foot position demonstrates balance.
26. **D** — The method of throwing was adjusted.
27. **D** — The comfortable reach demonstrates movement range.
28. **A** — Control means regulating where and how movement occurs.
29. **B** — A wider base can make it easier to maintain a steady position.
30. **B** — The child needs to regulate the landing and remain steady.
31. **C** — The amount of force is part of controlling the pass.
32. **B** — Strength relates to producing force; flexibility to movement range.
