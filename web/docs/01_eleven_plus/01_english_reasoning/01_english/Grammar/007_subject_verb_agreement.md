# Subject Verb Agreement

# ⭐ **What is Subject–Verb Agreement?**

Subject–Verb Agreement means:

👉 **The verb must match the subject in number.**

* **Singular subject → Singular verb (usually ends with –s)**
* **Plural subject → Plural verb (no –s)**

**Examples:**

* The dog **barks**. (singular)
* The dogs **bark**. (plural)

> **He / she / it = verb gets S**
> **I / you / we / they = verb has NO S**

---

# 📚 **RULE 1 — Singular & Plural Subjects**

### ✔ Singular → singular verb (+s)

* The cat **runs**.
* A girl **likes** music.

### ✔ Plural → plural verb (no s)

* The cats **run**.
* My friends **like** music.

---

# 📚 **RULE 2 — “He / She / It” always uses verb + S**

* He **plays**
* She **walks**
* It **bites**

---

# 📚 **RULE 3 — “I / You / We / They” behave like plural subjects**

* I **run**, not "runs"
* You **eat**, not "eats"

---

# 📚 **RULE 4 — Ignore Prepositional Phrases**

The subject is **never** in the phrase:

❌ The *basket of apples* **are** heavy.
✔ The **basket** of apples **is** heavy. (basket = subject)
Once you know the subject is basket (singular), you must use a singular verb.
Using the rules we discussed:
• The basket (singular) \(\rightarrow \) needs the singular verb ending in -s (is).
• The apples (plural) \(\rightarrow \) would need the plural verb (are).
Because you are weighing the basket as a single whole unit, you say "The basket is heavy." If you took the apples out and spread them on the table, you would say, "The apples are heavy."

Other examples:

* The group **of boys is** loud.
* The box **of toys has** fallen.

---

# 📚 **RULE 5 — Indefinite Pronouns**

These are **always singular**:

* everyone
* everybody
* anyone
* somebody
* nobody
* each
* every

✔ Everyone **is** here.
✔ Nobody **likes** homework.
✔ Each child **was** ready.

---

### Some can be plural:

* few
* many
* several
* both

✔ Many **were** waiting.
✔ Few **have** finished.

---

# 📚 **RULE 6 — Subjects joined by “and” are plural**

* Tom and Jerry **are** friends.
* A pen and a pencil **are** on the table.

### ❗Exception: When the pair is ONE idea

* Bread and butter **is** my favourite.
* Fish and chips **is** popular.

---

# 📚 **RULE 7 — “Either/Or” and “Neither/Nor” — verb matches the **closest** subject**

* Either the teacher **or the students are** coming.
* Either the students **or the teacher is** coming.

---

# 📚 **RULE 8 — Collective nouns**

These are singular:

* team
* family
* group
* crowd
* committee

✔ The team **is** winning.
✔ My family **is** travelling.

---

# 📚 **RULE 9 — Titles of books, films, etc. are singular**

✔ “Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire” **is** my favourite.
